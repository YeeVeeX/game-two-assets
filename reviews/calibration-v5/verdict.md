## Structured critique and cross-vendor review (Kimi K2.5, adversarial)

One consolidated verdict; every attack re-verified against artifacts and
pinned numbers before acceptance (models see text, not pixels):

1. **Q1 isolation "contaminated by the −6px snap" — REFUTED with the
   design, kernel adopted.** The snap is identical in A, R, and C by
   construction, so it cannot produce the R-vs-A differential; A after
   the same snap stays invisible, R after the same snap reads — the
   difference is the pose, which is the isolation working, not failing.
   ADOPTED: mid-span readability (a player who looks after the snap) is
   carried by the pose alone — that is exactly what the per-tick claim
   covers; anything the snap adds is temporal and unclaimed.
2. **Q2 floor honesty — REFUTED WITH NUMBERS.** The attack inverted the
   metric: popping_pct is distinctness (100·XOR/union), so r0-vs-a0 27.94
   > banked a0-vs-k0 27.18 means MORE separation than the banked
   same-facing precedent, not more confusability; "v3's a0-vs-k0 is
   cross-faction banked" is fabricated (v3 measured the same creature,
   same facing); and "1.67pt headroom is below the 1.58pt precedent" is
   backwards arithmetic. ADOPTED from the same answer: the honest
   disclosure that r0↔a0 margins straddle the per-facing a0↔k0 precedents
   (down 0.76pt wider, right 2.37pt tighter than that facing's pair) —
   the thinnest margin class on the sheet, disclosed in the scores.
3. **Q3 C-rejection circularity — PARTIALLY ACCEPTED.** The runtime
   severity of the inversion (misparse rates under play) is untested and
   is now recorded as predicted-not-proven. REFUTED as grounds to prefer
   C: the sheet-level failure is static and visible — C reuses one pose
   for two opposite states in the same action (its counter-argument that
   the −3 vs 0 offset separates them runs directly into the v2-banked
   offset-subthreshold finding). R's preference rests on state-uniqueness
   evidence, not on sunk pixels; the decision rule pre-committed to C
   winning had it passed line 2.
4. **Q4 asymmetric-slump risks — ACCEPTED, recorded.** (a) Moving
   recovery: walking is legal during recovery, so r0→walk-frame
   transitions (leg teleport at walk-init) need a pose-priority decision
   — elevated into the integration findings. (b) Facing-mirror
   handedness: only down/right exist in the banked set; if a future
   facing set mirrors sprites, the slump's lateral direction must be
   re-verified per facing. (c) Enemy-side reuse re-verification (banked
   v4 finding) extends to r0. The "below your own precedent" headroom
   claim within this question was arithmetic error (see Q2).
5. **Q5 ready-again beat — ACCEPTED as scope correction (the v4 Q1
   class).** A static sheet cannot prove a pose-only single-tick change
   registers as a beat at 60 tps. Rubric line 3's PASS is scoped to what
   the sheet proves: the boundary exists, is single-tick recognizable
   (37% silhouette change at constant position), and is absent in A. Live
   perceptibility joins the runtime replay item.
6. **Q6 unthought risks — two adopted.** (a) **The locked window is
   larger than the recovery state:** exhaust holds the attack lock ~35

## Decision

**R WINS at the pre-registered artifact scope — the recovery hypothesis is
answered YES at asset level:** a distinct follow-through pose (r0) held
for the pinned 8 recovery ticks at offset 0 makes the locked state
readable against ready-idle on the lane-B body at native 1x, distinct
from every walk frame, the coil, and the strike, under the identity
ceiling — and it beats both incumbents under identical pinned timing:

- **R beats A (readability):** A's recovery span is pixel-identical to
  ready-idle for all eight ticks; R's is per-tick distinguishable and
  gives the action a visible completion.
- **R beats C (semantic correctness):** C fails rubric line 2 at sheet
  level — reusing a0 makes one pose encode loading and spent in the same
  action, the pre-registered grammar inversion. Zero pixels was not worth
  a two-faced state.

What this sprint does **not** claim: live-60tps perceptibility of the
pose-only ready-again beat, misparse rates for C under play, or any
temporal integration — those are runtime replay-capture questions,
blocked by the one-way boundary. The 133.3 ms recovery hold sits 16.7 ms
under the KB ~150 ms follow-through reference: duration is engine-owned,
adequate, and was never this sprint's risk.

Status per contract: **selectable candidates only.** No integration —
v18 is open in game-two; the one-way boundary and every
runtime-integration stop condition in `docs/asset-contract.md` hold. The
five-state lane-B grammar (idle, walk, coil, strike, settle) is now
complete and closed at asset level: v0–v2 keys, v3 spatial, v4 temporal
structure, v5 completion readability.
