## Decision

**B WINS BOTH BOUNDARIES at the pre-registered artifact scope — the
transition-smoothing hypothesis is answered YES at asset level, with
per-boundary sub-verdicts:**

- **Onset (w0): BANK.** The idle→a0 discontinuity decomposes into two
  steps of roughly half magnitude (16.48/17.25 down, 19.69/20.83
  right vs 31.10/36.57), directionally consistent (byte-exact head at
  half-translation, monotone mass, bulge/compression beginning), with
  the nearest-neighbor guard defeating the pre-registered k0-collision
  at static scope in both facings. Rubric lines 1, 3, 4, 5 pass
  sheet-wide.
- **Settle (s0): BANK.** The k0→r0 pose swap decomposes (15.38/16.18
  down, 21.31/14.98 right vs 27.12/29.90) with the gape closing exactly
  on the first settle tick and the collapse completing in r0; the −6px
  return remains engine-owned and now lands on a half-step pose change.
  Rubric lines 2, 3, 4, 5 pass sheet-wide.
- **The release is untouched and machine-proven identical in A and B**
  (27.18/31.97, +9px) — and B's smoothing corrects the incumbent's
  salience inversion (A's onset pop out-jumped the strike pose-wise;
  in B the strike is the largest attack-boundary pose jump AND the only
  displaced one).

What this sprint does **not** claim: temporal fusion at 60 tps (whether
one-tick bridges read as motion rather than strobe), duration
sufficiency of the shortened 66.7 ms coil hold, live attention capture
by the release, or any runtime integration — those are runtime
replay-capture questions, blocked by the one-way boundary. The
per-boundary sub-verdicts are asset-level: the in-between keys exist,
bridge measurably, read as bridges at native 1x, and weaken nothing
